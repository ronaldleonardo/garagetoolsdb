The GENIUS5 is the ultimate all-in-one solution for battery charging, maintenance, and restoration, delivering 34% more power in a 65% smaller design compared to its predecessor, the G3500. Designed for 6-volt and 12-volt lead-acid (AGM, gel, flooded, and deep-cycle) and lithium-ion (LiFePO4) batteries (up to 120 amp-hours), it is compatible with a vast array of vehicles, such as cars, automobiles, motorcycles, dirt bikes, scooters, mopeds, lawn mowers, ATVs, UTVs, tractors, trucks, SUVs, RVs, campers, trailers, boats, PWCs, jet skis, snowmobiles, golf carts, classic cars,and more; including automotive brands like BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, Bentley, Maserati, Lexus, Audi, Ford, Chevrolet, Corvette, Cadillac, Prius, Toyota, Subaru, Nissan, powersport vehicles such as Harley-Davidson, Honda, Yamaha, Kawasaki, Ducati, Indian, KTM, Husqvarna, Vespa, Polaris, Seadoo, as well as lawn and garden vehicles such as John Deere, Kubota, Toro and more. Featuring an integrated thermal sensor, the GENIUS5 adjusts its charging profile based on ambient temperature, ensuring optimal performance by eliminating the risk of overcharging in hot climates and undercharging in cold ones. With precision pulse charging, the GENIUS5 restores battery health by reversing sulfation and acid stratification, improving performance and extending battery life. The package includes a 5A charger (120-240VAC), 72-inch AC cable, 80-inch DC cable with battery clamps, and integrated eyelet terminals, mounting bracket and screws, all backed by a three-year warranty. Proudly designed in the USA, the GENIUS5 combines power, reliability, and convenience in a compact design to keep your batteries ready for whatever lies ahead.

I will condense a bunch of sources and data into a few concepts you can "bank" on:

1). Auto alternators will de-sulfate batteries of "soft" sulfate covering lead battery plates. This begins to happen whenever your alternator is not charging the battery.

2) Alternators will NOT de-sulfate batteries where plates have become covered with "hard" (crystalized sulfate). It takes high levels of voltage, "pulsed" to accomplish this. This function is known as "recovery" or "forced" de-sulfation. Recovery de-sulfation must occur at voltages higher than 15 volts.

3). The Noco chargers have a "recovery" or "forced" mode for a reason. It is a manual selection and before making that selection they recommend you disconnect BOTH battery terminals. Removing the battery might not be a bad idea, either. Performing a "recovery" or "forced" de-sulfation with terminals connected puts anything in your auto's electronics (CPU, sensors, etc) at HIGH RISK of being fried due to the high voltages over 15 volts. This data is not stressed anywhere on the Internet that I can find.

4). The Noco chargers make the high de-sulfation modes "selected", rather than automatic (convenience) because you will know your car's electronics are not being exposed to voltages higher than those already being supplied by the alternator. That is a Noco design concept and a huge for "Sleeping Well At Night" (SWAN).

5). What is NOT known by most of us and difficult to locate, is the "de-sulfation" voltage of any one of the other dozens of smart chargers out there, e.g., Battery Tender, Black & Decker. Yuasa de-sulfation voltage will not exceed 14.08 volts, according to a company support specialist. Still, is the de-sulfation mode supplying a voltage level higher than 15 volts? If it is, you're putting your auto electronics at risk without disconnecting the battery terminals -- which, of course, directly conflicts with the convenience you're seeking when "setting and forgetting". Does your smart charger tell you what THAT de-sulfation voltage is?

6). So does your smart battery charger "soft-desulfate" or "hard-desulfate"? The difference is the voltage and duration of pulses.

Most of us won't let our batteries get in such bad shape. We'll replace them rather than attempt to wring out every possible CCA or every last Reserve Capacity hour.
